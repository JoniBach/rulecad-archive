# rulecad archive

## Experiments

- **[An arm that draws, then lifts, then a leg: checked before it's printed](experiments/robots/)** (2026-11-10): A SCARA arm sized from its task, the same parts upright with a gripper and a Z axis, and a leg from the arm's gearbox, with reach, speed, an error budget, bearing and link loads, wiring, a shopping list and a build page from one assembly. Predictions only so far: nothing has been printed or moved. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/robots/)
- **[What to print to test the robots: benchmarks, then the arm, then the leg](experiments/robots-print-list/)** (2026-11-10): The print list and measurements that settle the robot work's three open tests: the bench parts first, then the arm that draws, the same arm lifting, and a leg on a stand, with what each one needs and what it decides. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/robots-print-list/)
- **[Benchmarks for a robot joint: every estimate, measured](experiments/benchmarks/)** (2026-11-09): Small parts with plain first layers that measure what a printed robot joint rests on: fits, backlash, a stepper's real torque, what a mesh passes on, and a 38:1 shoulder's play, twist and repeatability. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/benchmarks/)
- **[Motors from rules: mounted, driven, and checked before they're bought](experiments/motors/)** (2026-11-09): @rulecad/motors: steppers, gear motors, servos and a brushless motor from their datasheets, with mounts, D-bores and torque curves; motors driving joints in assemblies, checked for torque, speed, holding and heat. A motorised focus slider and a robot shoulder through a 38:1 compound planetary; measurements to come. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/motors/)
- **[Gears from rules: spur to bevel, checked before they're printed](experiments/gears/)** (2026-11-08): @rulecad/gears: involute spur gears, racks, rings, helical and herringbone gears, planetaries, worms and bevels from their module and teeth; meshed in phase in assemblies, measured from their drawn teeth, and their teeth analysed by finite elements. A sampler plate to print, a focus slider and a spool winder; measurements to come. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/gears/)
- **[Launcher trigger: catching a strong spring and letting it go at once](experiments/launcher-trigger/)** (2026-11-03): Work in progress on the in-line launcher's catch and trigger: a sear whose hinge carries the spring's pull as tension, four layouts tried and what each taught, and the two-stage catch being built next. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/launcher-trigger/)
- **[In-line launcher: a cut through the stem's bridge and floor](experiments/launcher-inline-section/)** (2026-11-02): Down the middle of the stem: the bridge prints over it with a 0.6 mm gap, and the stem arches over the floor at 45°. Nothing joins the frame to the pusher.. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/launcher-inline-section/)
- **[Mechanisms solved as they would behave: beams that bend, parts that touch, a hand, and a shot](experiments/mechanism-physics/)** (2026-11-02): A large-deflection beam solver, contact, force curves, firing in time and a wave spring, built overnight for printed compliant mechanisms; a key with a tactile click and a wave spring as test pieces, and what it found in the launcher. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/mechanism-physics/)
- **[Flat launcher action: a mechanism printed on its side, checked through its whole cycle](experiments/flat-launcher/)** (2026-11-01): The disc launcher's action redrawn as one flat print, the way working printed blasters are made, with new checks for print-in-place gaps, jams, catches that hold and hard stops, and an animation of the cycle. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/flat-launcher/)
- **[Disc launcher: a toy pistol whose springs, sear, trigger and feeder are printed flexures](experiments/launcher/)** (2026-10-31): A leaf bow, a snap-hook sear, a trigger on a living hinge and a serpentine feeder, all sized from one rule set; first as one optimised body, then as two cartridges pinned into a frame. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/launcher/)
- **[Bin: a lattice wall for the X1C's whole build volume](experiments/bin/)** (2026-10-30): At 5% four legs become pointed arches; at 8% diagonals brace them and the rim is reached: settled, print-ready, 1.06 kg. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/bin/)
- **[Flexure samplers: twenty-two flexures and mechanisms to measure](experiments/flexures/)** (2026-10-30): Four printed plates with every flexure in the library and its predicted stiffness and travel; measured, they give the material its strain limit. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/flexures/)
- **[Guitar hanger: a wall mount grown from two screws to a cradle](experiments/guitar/)** (2026-10-30): A Telecaster hanger grown from two screw bosses to two arm pads; the second attempt keeps the neck's channel and the screw columns empty, settled in 57 minutes, every check passing. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/guitar/)
- **[The force inverter: a mechanism grown from a block](experiments/inverter/)** (2026-10-30): The first generated compliant mechanism; its point hinges, and the robust formulation that gives it real ones. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/inverter/)
- **[Tree stand: from stool to goblet to Y to tree](experiments/tree/)** (2026-10-30): Where the loads sit decides whether a part branches; finer cells with bridges gave a lattice canopy, stiffer and print-ready. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/tree/)
- **[Bracket bench: four wall brackets of equal mass](experiments/bracket-bench/)** (2026-10-28): Optimised brackets predicted 1.5 times as stiff as a plain web; what printing without supports costs. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/bracket-bench/)
- **[L-bracket: stiffest against stress-limited](experiments/lbracket/)** (2026-10-28): Stress limits cut the peak stress by 46% for the same material. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/lbracket/)
- **[Lever bench: four levers of equal mass](experiments/lever-bench/)** (2026-10-28): The optimised lever predicted 27 times as stiff as a lightened plate. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/lever-bench/)
- **[Twisted tube: the first showcase part](experiments/twist/)** (2026-10-28): Printed in one piece at the second attempt, but with flat blocks and icicles that need supports. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/twist/)
- **[Verification: how sure are the predictions?](experiments/verification/)** (2026-10-28): Finer cells showed the first bracket predictions were 15–35% too flexible. [On the site](https://jonibach.github.io/rulecad-archive/experiments/verification/)

- **Bring in links shared as Gists:** `node import-gists.mjs`.
