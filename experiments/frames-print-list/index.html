What to print to test fixings and frames

21 November 2026. Screw holes, inserts, printed threads, slot fits, the springiness of a join and the stiffness of a printed beam are all estimates in the kit until they're measured. This page lists the four coupons that measure them, in order, and then the frame they lead up to. Issue 276 and the frames umbrella, issue 240, stay open until that's done.

Each link opens the model in 3D, with its parts to download. Print in PLA at 0.2 mm layers unless it says otherwise. The row numbers refer to the measuring sheet, which says how to take each measurement and gives the exact command that writes it back.

Together that comes to about 390 g and 7 hours of printing, counting the fixings and beam coupons twice (PLA and PETG). npx cad plates benchmarks/joint-test and npx cad plates benchmarks/beam-test write the plates for 3 and 4.

Optional: npx cad beams benchmarks/joint-test --jigs gives six jigs for cutting, drilling and tapping the rig's beams (109 g). They're worth printing if you have no drill press or tap guide.

The fixings coupon decides one open question: rows M8P and M8S compare the two ways of printing a thread, flanks laid back to 45° (the kit's default) against the standard's 60°. Row 28 says how to judge them.

The measuring sheet's "What you need" table has the full list, including each beam's drilling.

Writing the results back

Each measurement goes back with one command, and every prediction that rests on it moves. For example:

``sh npx cad measured benchmarks/fixings-test x1c.pilot_m3=2.9 --source "fixings coupon, PLA" npx cad measured benchmarks/joint-test c1.drop=0.35 npx cad measured benchmarks/beam-test k1.sag=0.6 ``

(These figures are examples. Use what you measure.)

Then: build and measure a frame (issue 276)

With the coupons written back, build chapter 16's frame, a box of 2020 with a moving axis. Its 17 printed parts fit one plate (124 g, about 2¼ hours), and npx cad beams gives the cut list, the drilling sheets and eight jigs. Measure the two diagonals, the sag at mid-span under a known mass, the lowest frequency (tap it and record the sound on a phone), the axis's travel, and the printed parts' mass. Write each one back with npx cad measured. The table of predicted against measured then goes on this archive.

Chapter 17's printed case is the frame to build if you want to test printed beams in a real build rather than the coupon. It needs no aluminium.
